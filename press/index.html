A virtual pet game where the shop is behind a maths question

November 2026. Noodle Pets, a virtual pet game for children aged four and up, is out now on the App Store for iPhone. It is free, carries no advertising, and collects nothing about the children who play it.

Download on the App Store

Children adopt one of thirteen pets, name it, choose its colour, and look after it day by day: feeding, washing, playing and putting it to bed. The pets grow over real days rather than play sessions, so a pet raised for a month looks and behaves differently from one adopted yesterday. There are mini games, a dressing-up wardrobe, a trip through the solar system that teaches the planets, and a microphone the pet listens through before repeating what it heard in a comically high voice.

The part that will interest parents sits underneath all of that. Every screen in Noodle Pets that could cost money, and every link that leaves the app, is locked behind a multiplication question. A child who taps the shop is shown something like “14 × 5 = ?” and a number pad. Get it wrong and the shop does not open. There is no chat, no social feed, no advertising network, no analytics, and no account to create. Saved games live on the child’s own device and in the family’s own private iCloud folder, where the developer cannot see them.

It is a game for children, but it was not built only for them. Plenty of families cannot have a real animal. A landlord who says no, an allergy, a flat too small, a parent who is simply not ready yet: the reasons are ordinary and they are everywhere, and the child who wants a dog still wants a dog. Noodle Pets does not pretend to replace one. It gives that child something of their own to be responsible for.

It also works as a rehearsal. Anyone weighing up a real animal and quietly unsure whether they could keep up with one can find out here first, for nothing, before a shelter or a breeder is involved. A pet in Noodle Pets gets hungry whether or not you open the app, which is the honest part. And adults play it for themselves: something that notices you have been away is decent company at any age.

“Not every family can have a real pet. A landlord, an allergy, a flat that is too small. And plenty of people want one but are not sure they could look after it. You can find that out here first, for free, before an actual animal is involved.”

Jad Yazbeck, creator of Noodle Pets

Noodle Pets was built by one person, Jad Yazbeck, an independent designer and developer, over the course of a year.
